## Discovery question one — ask this before any number is said

**"How many service requests come in outside your office hours, and what happens to them now?"**

The entire premise is that requests arriving Friday evening sit until Monday morning with no
context attached. I do not know that this is a problem for Kennedy's. I am guessing.

If the answer is small, or mostly people who also call the next morning, I do not argue. I ask the
second question instead: **"When your office opens Monday, how much time goes into working out
what each of these people actually needs?"** That is a problem even at low volume, and it is the
one the structured intake and the prep sheets solve.

If neither lands, the honest move is to say the tool may not be worth it to them, leave the demo
link live, and go. A pitch I talk myself out of costs one morning.

## If they say it's too expensive

Do not answer immediately. Let the silence sit. Then pick one. Never lead with a discount.

**1. Cut scope, not price.**
"I can get to $7,600. Here's what comes out: the impact calculator, the drag-and-drop board becomes
a dropdown, one round of revisions instead of two, one week of tuning instead of two, and one
training session instead of two. The intake, the triage ranking with the reasons, and the drafting
all stay, because that's the part that changes your Monday. Does that trade work?"

**2. Change the shape of the money.**
"The number is the number, but it doesn't all have to be this month. Four payments across the
build. Or phase it: $3,200 now for the intake, live in two to three weeks, then $6,200 for the
office desk before March. That's $9,400 total, $500 more than doing it in one pass, because the
second phase repeats some of the setup and testing. I'll hold both numbers through March."

Write those three figures down in front of him. Ambiguity here is where a good deal turns into a
dispute later.

**3. Find out what "expensive" means.**
"Can I ask which part doesn't feel worth it, the number itself or not knowing whether it'll work?"

Usually the second, which is a risk problem, not a price problem, and risk has answers that cost
me nothing: the deposit buys a defined first milestone he owns either way, the five-day acceptance
window, the source and data in his name from day one, and the demo he is already holding.

What I do not do: drop the price on the spot with nothing asked in return.
